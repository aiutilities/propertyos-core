# PropertyOS Code of Conduct

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Status | Active |
| Version | 1.0 |
| Effective date | 25 July 2026 |
| Steward | Cogzidel Technologies Pvt. Ltd. |
| Applies to | PropertyOS repositories, discussions, events, communication channels and community spaces |
| Authority | PropertyOS Constitution |

---

## 1. Purpose

PropertyOS is built as an open-source platform and community.

A strong community requires more than technical skill.

It requires respect, responsibility, fairness and trust.

This Code of Conduct defines the minimum behaviour expected from everyone participating in PropertyOS.

---

## 2. Our Commitment

We are committed to making participation in PropertyOS a respectful and harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of:

- age
- body size
- caste
- disability
- education
- ethnicity
- gender identity
- gender expression
- level of experience
- nationality
- personal appearance
- race
- religion
- sex characteristics
- sexual orientation
- socioeconomic status
- language
- geographic location
- professional background
- technical preference

We value diversity of experience and thought.

We expect every participant to help create an environment in which people can contribute safely and meaningfully.

---

## 3. Expected Behaviour

Community members are expected to:

- communicate respectfully
- assume good faith where reasonable
- provide constructive feedback
- focus criticism on ideas and work, not people
- acknowledge mistakes
- correct misinformation
- respect privacy
- respect contributor time
- follow project governance
- avoid unnecessary disruption
- disclose relevant conflicts of interest
- accept reasonable maintainer decisions
- help newcomers understand the project
- protect users and the community from harm
- comply with applicable law

Disagreement is normal.

Disrespect is not.

---

## 4. Constructive Technical Discussion

Technical discussion should be:

- specific
- evidence-based
- proportionate
- relevant
- reviewable
- open to correction

Useful feedback explains:

- what is wrong
- why it matters
- what evidence supports the concern
- what alternatives may exist

Avoid:

- dismissive statements
- personal attacks
- sarcasm aimed at individuals
- status-based arguments
- repeated demands after a decision is explained
- derailing unrelated discussions
- misrepresenting another person’s position

---

## 5. Unacceptable Behaviour

Unacceptable behaviour includes:

- harassment
- discrimination
- intimidation
- threats
- stalking
- sustained disruption
- personal insults
- sexualized language or imagery
- unwanted sexual attention
- deliberate misgendering
- caste-based abuse
- racist or religious abuse
- doxxing
- publishing private information
- impersonation
- fraudulent claims of authority
- malicious reporting
- coordinated harassment
- retaliation
- knowingly false accusations
- encouraging self-harm
- glorifying violence
- distributing malware
- attempting unauthorized access
- exploiting vulnerabilities against users
- repeatedly ignoring maintainer boundaries

This list is not exhaustive.

---

## 6. Security and Privacy

Security-sensitive information must be handled responsibly.

Do not:

- disclose unpatched vulnerabilities publicly
- publish credentials
- publish private keys
- publish customer data
- publish personal data
- publish production logs containing secrets
- attempt unauthorized access
- test exploits against systems without permission
- use project channels to distribute malicious code

Security reports must follow `SECURITY.md` once published.

Until then, report vulnerabilities privately to the project steward.

---

## 7. Maintainer Responsibilities

Maintainers are responsible for:

- clarifying expected behaviour
- responding to reports
- applying this Code consistently
- protecting reporter privacy
- avoiding conflicts of interest
- documenting serious enforcement decisions where appropriate
- distinguishing disagreement from misconduct
- acting proportionately
- protecting the project from bad-faith disruption
- avoiding abuse of authority

Maintainer status does not exempt anyone from this Code.

---

## 8. Scope

This Code applies in:

- GitHub repositories
- issues
- pull requests
- discussions
- review comments
- community forums
- chat platforms
- email lists
- events
- video calls
- social-media interactions
- official training
- official workshops
- official partner channels
- spaces where someone represents PropertyOS

It may also apply to conduct outside project spaces when that conduct creates a credible risk to community safety or project integrity.

---

## 9. Representation of the Project

A person represents PropertyOS when they:

- act as a maintainer
- speak on behalf of the project
- use an official title
- use official communication channels
- organize an official event
- represent an authorized community group
- claim official endorsement
- use official PropertyOS branding

Representatives must follow:

- this Code of Conduct
- the PropertyOS Constitution
- `TRADEMARKS.md`
- project governance
- applicable law

---

## 10. Reporting Conduct Concerns

Do not report sensitive conduct concerns in a public issue.

A report should include, where possible:

- what happened
- when it happened
- where it happened
- who was involved
- available evidence
- relevant links
- witnesses
- immediate safety concerns
- whether confidentiality is requested

A dedicated private conduct-reporting address will be published as part of the governance programme.

Until then, reports should be sent privately to Cogzidel Technologies Pvt. Ltd. through an appropriate confidential channel.

---

## 11. Confidentiality

Reports will be handled as confidentially as reasonably possible.

Information may be shared only with people required to:

- assess the report
- protect participants
- investigate the conduct
- obtain legal advice
- comply with law
- implement enforcement

Absolute confidentiality cannot be guaranteed where disclosure is legally required or necessary to address immediate safety risks.

---

## 12. Good-Faith Reporting

Good-faith reports are protected.

Retaliation against someone for making or supporting a good-faith report is prohibited.

Knowingly false, malicious or fabricated reports may themselves violate this Code.

An unsubstantiated report is not automatically a malicious report.

---

## 13. Enforcement Principles

Enforcement should be:

- fair
- proportionate
- timely
- consistent
- documented where appropriate
- respectful of privacy
- focused on safety and project health

Relevant factors may include:

- severity
- frequency
- intent
- impact
- power imbalance
- prior behaviour
- willingness to correct
- risk to users
- risk to the community
- risk to the project

---

## 14. Enforcement Actions

Possible actions include:

### 14.1 Informal correction

A private explanation or request to change behaviour.

### 14.2 Formal warning

A written warning identifying the conduct and expected correction.

### 14.3 Content removal

Removal of comments, issues, pull requests, messages or other content.

### 14.4 Participation limits

Temporary restrictions on posting, reviewing, attending or interacting.

### 14.5 Temporary suspension

Temporary removal from community spaces or contributor activity.

### 14.6 Role removal

Removal of maintainer, moderator or representative status.

### 14.7 Permanent ban

Permanent exclusion from project-controlled spaces.

### 14.8 Referral

Referral to platform administrators, employers, event organizers, legal counsel or authorities where appropriate.

Not every case requires progressive escalation.

Severe conduct may result in immediate removal or permanent exclusion.

---

## 15. Appeals

A person subject to formal enforcement may request review when:

- relevant evidence was not considered
- there was a material factual error
- there was a conflict of interest
- enforcement was disproportionate
- procedure was not followed

An appeal must be respectful and focused on the decision.

An appeal is not an opportunity to harass reporters or repeat prohibited conduct.

The review outcome may affirm, modify or reverse the original action.

---

## 16. Conflicts of Interest

Anyone handling a report should disclose a material conflict of interest.

Where practical, the matter should be reassigned.

Conflicts may include:

- close personal relationships
- direct reporting relationships
- business disputes
- active litigation
- personal involvement in the incident
- financial interests
- prior public hostility

---

## 17. Community Leadership

Maintainers and community leaders are expected to model:

- humility
- patience
- transparency
- restraint
- accountability
- fairness
- stewardship

Authority exists to serve the project and community.

It must not be used for personal status, retaliation or intimidation.

---

## 18. Contributor Recognition

Recognition should be based on contribution, responsibility and conduct.

No one is entitled to influence solely because of:

- seniority
- title
- employer
- wealth
- popularity
- follower count
- technical reputation
- prior contribution volume

Respectful behaviour remains mandatory for everyone.

---

## 19. Cultural and Language Differences

PropertyOS is intended to serve a global community.

Participants should recognize that:

- communication styles differ
- English proficiency varies
- cultural expectations differ
- tone may be misread
- directness may be interpreted differently

Ask for clarification before assuming harmful intent where reasonable.

This does not excuse harassment, discrimination or threats.

---

## 20. Accessibility

Community participation should be accessible whenever practical.

Participants and organizers should consider:

- readable documents
- captions
- accessible event tools
- keyboard navigation
- clear language
- time-zone diversity
- asynchronous participation
- reasonable accommodation

Accessibility feedback should be treated respectfully.

---

## 21. Commercial Participation

Companies, consultants, vendors, hosting providers and commercial plugin authors are welcome.

Commercial participants must not:

- misrepresent official status
- dominate community channels through promotion
- conceal material conflicts
- manipulate project decisions
- pressure contributors
- use private user information improperly
- violate the trademark policy

Commercial success does not create special immunity from community standards.

---

## 22. Artificial Intelligence Use

AI tools may assist with code, documentation, review, translation and community work.

Participants remain responsible for AI-assisted contributions.

Do not use AI to:

- fabricate evidence
- impersonate people
- generate harassment
- expose confidential data
- conceal plagiarism
- flood project channels
- bypass review
- create misleading attribution

AI-generated or AI-assisted work must meet the same quality, security and licensing standards as other contributions.

---

## 23. Legal Rights

This Code is a community-governance document.

It does not replace applicable law, employment rights, contractual rights or legal remedies.

Nothing in this Code requires a person to give up legal rights.

---

## 24. Updates

This Code may evolve with the community.

Material changes should be:

- documented
- versioned
- publicly available
- aligned with the PropertyOS Constitution
- applied prospectively whenever practical
