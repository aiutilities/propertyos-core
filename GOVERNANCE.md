## 1. Purpose

This document defines how PropertyOS is governed in practice.

The PropertyOS Constitution establishes the project's enduring principles.

This governance policy translates those principles into operational rules for:

- project authority;
- stewardship;
- maintainership;
- technical decisions;
- release decisions;
- security decisions;
- contributor participation;
- voting;
- conflict resolution;
- policy amendments;
- succession and continuity.

This document does not replace the Constitution.

Where this document conflicts with the Constitution, the Constitution prevails.

---

## 2. Project Identity

PropertyOS is:

> The Open Source AI-Native Operating System for Properties, Workspaces and Hospitality.

The long-term vision of PropertyOS is:

> Build infrastructure for the physical world.

PropertyOS is created, maintained and stewarded by
**Cogzidel Technologies Pvt. Ltd.**

The project is designed as:

- plugin-first;
- API-first;
- event-driven;
- AI-native;
- provider-agnostic;
- constitution-driven;
- open-source;
- extensible;
- observable;
- security-conscious.

Governance decisions must preserve this identity.

## 3. Governance Principles

PropertyOS governance follows these principles:

1. The Constitution is the highest project authority.
2. Stewardship must protect the long-term mission.
3. Technical decisions must be evidence-based.
4. Product complexity should remain inside the platform.
5. Human authorization remains required for consequential decisions.
6. Security and release integrity take priority over convenience.
7. Project authority must be explicit.
8. Important decisions must be documented.
9. Maintainer powers must be limited by scope.
10. Contributors should have a clear path to increased responsibility.
11. Governance must remain understandable to the public.
12. Commercial interests must not silently override project principles.
13. AI may assist governance but may not replace accountable human authority.
14. Stable releases require controlled authorization.
15. Project continuity must not depend on one undocumented individual.

---

## 4. Governance Hierarchy

PropertyOS governance follows this hierarchy:

1. PropertyOS Constitution
2. Applicable law
3. Open-source licence
4. Governance policy
5. Security policy
6. Maintainer policy
7. Release governance
8. Marketplace governance
9. AI governance
10. Technical standards
11. Architecture decisions
12. Repository procedures
13. Individual maintainer decisions

A lower-level authority may not override a higher-level authority.

When two documents appear to conflict, maintainers must:

1. identify the conflict;
2. pause the affected decision where practical;
3. consult the higher authority;
4. document the interpretation;
5. amend the lower-level document when required.

- mission;
- product identity;
- architectural direction;
- foundational principles;
- human accountability;
- openness;
- extensibility;
- platform integrity;
- long-term stewardship.

Routine governance decisions must not be used to bypass the constitutional
amendment process.

**Cogzidel Technologies Pvt. Ltd.**

- trademark integrity;

security, privacy or legal obligations prevent publication.

## 22. Decision Categories

PropertyOS decisions are classified as:

### Routine decisions

Examples:

- typo corrections;
- issue triage;
- documentation clarification;
- non-breaking maintenance;
- test improvements;
- dependency maintenance without material impact.

Routine decisions may be approved by an authorized maintainer.

### Significant decisions

Examples:

- cross-module architecture;
- public API changes;
- supported-version changes;
- release-process changes;
- official plugin or theme policy;
- security-control changes;
- AI action-policy changes;
- deprecations;
- governance-role changes.

Significant decisions require documented review.

### Constitutional decisions

Examples:

- changing the project mission;
- changing foundational principles;
- abandoning open-source status;
- materially changing stewardship;
- removing human accountability;
- changing the constitutional hierarchy.

Constitutional decisions require the constitutional amendment process.

---

## 23. Decision-Making Model

The project prefers consensus.

Consensus does not require unanimous enthusiasm.

Consensus means:

- material objections were heard;
- evidence was considered;
- risks were documented;
- alternatives were evaluated;
- the decision is acceptable enough to proceed;
- dissent is recorded where appropriate.

When consensus cannot be reached, the applicable voting or escalation process
may be used.

---

## 24. Proposal Process

A significant proposal should include:

- title;
- problem statement;
- context;
- proposed decision;
- alternatives;
- compatibility impact;
- security impact;
- operational impact;
- migration impact;
- documentation impact;
- ecosystem impact;
- AI impact where relevant;
- implementation plan;
- rollback or reversal approach;
- responsible owners.

The project may require an Architecture Decision Record, policy change, issue,
discussion or pull request depending on the proposal.

---

## 25. Request for Comments

Major proposals may use a Request for Comments, or RFC, process.

An RFC should provide sufficient time for:

- maintainer review;
- contributor feedback;
- affected-owner review;
- security review;
- operational review;
- documentation review.

The review period may be shortened for urgent security or legal matters.

An RFC is not accepted merely because no one commented.

An authorized decision must still be recorded.

---

## 26. Voting Eligibility

Voting rights belong only to currently authorized voting members of the
applicable body.

Voting status must be documented.

Contributors, sponsors, vendors, customers and employees do not automatically
receive voting rights.

A person holding multiple roles receives only one vote in the same decision
unless a policy explicitly states otherwise.

AI systems do not receive votes.

---

## 27. Quorum

Unless another approved policy defines a stricter requirement:

- quorum requires more than half of eligible voting members;
- abstentions count toward quorum;
- recused members do not count toward quorum for that decision;
- absent members do not count as votes.

A body with fewer than three eligible members should prefer steward review over
formal majority voting.

---

## 28. Voting Thresholds

Unless another approved policy defines otherwise:

### Routine governance vote

Requires a simple majority of votes cast.

### Significant governance vote

Requires at least two-thirds of votes cast.

### Maintainer appointment or removal

Requires at least two-thirds of votes cast and steward confirmation.

### Constitutional amendment

Requires the constitutional threshold defined by the Constitution or, when not
otherwise specified:

- at least three-quarters of eligible constitutional voters;
- explicit steward approval;
- a documented public rationale;
- a reasonable public review period.

A tie does not approve a proposal.

---

## 29. Abstention and Recusal

A member should abstain when they lack sufficient information.

A member must recuse themselves when a material conflict of interest could
reasonably affect impartiality.

Recusal should be recorded.

A recused member must not:

- vote;
- pressure other voters;
- privately control the outcome;
- access restricted information beyond what their other role requires.

Recusal does not imply wrongdoing.

---

Conflicts may include:

- financial interests;
- employment relationships;
- vendor relationships;
- customer contracts;
- family relationships;
- competitive interests;
- private extension ownership;
- personal disputes;
- security-report involvement;
- legal proceedings.

Relevant conflicts must be disclosed to the appropriate governance body.

The project may require:

- recusal;
- independent review;
- additional approval;
- public disclosure;
- limitation of authority.

Commercial sponsorship does not remove conflict-of-interest obligations.

---

## 31. Release Authority

Official release authority is separate from code approval.

An official release requires:

- approved release scope;
- required test evidence;
- required audit evidence;
- migration review where applicable;
- security review where applicable;
- documentation completeness;
- artifact-integrity verification;
- release-manager confirmation;
- authorized release approval.

Only designated release authorities may approve publication of an official
PropertyOS release.

Release authority must be recorded in `MAINTAINERS.md`.

---

## 32. Stable Releases

A stable release represents an official project commitment.

Stable releases must:

- satisfy release criteria;
- preserve compatibility commitments;
- contain approved source;
- include accurate release notes;
- include verified artifacts;
- include checksums;
- follow version policy;
- identify known limitations;
- preserve licence and notices.

No individual may publish an official stable release solely because they have
technical access to the release infrastructure.

---

## 33. Emergency Releases

An emergency release may be authorized when delay creates material risk.

Examples include:

- actively exploited vulnerabilities;
- release-artifact compromise;
- credential compromise;
- data-integrity defects;
- severe operational regressions;
- legal injunctions;
- ecosystem-wide breakage.

Emergency releases may use an accelerated process.

They must still preserve:

- accountable authorization;
- minimum review;
- scope control;
- audit evidence;
- artifact integrity;
- post-release review.

Emergency authority expires when the immediate condition ends.

---

## 34. Security Authority

Security maintainers may recommend temporary actions including:

- private remediation;
- restricted repository access;
- package revocation;
- key rotation;
- credential rotation;
- release suspension;
- advisory embargo;
- emergency patching.

Actions with material project-wide impact require steward or designated
security-authority approval unless immediate action is necessary to prevent
ongoing harm.

Emergency security actions must be documented after stabilization.

---

## 35. Repository Authority

Repository administration includes:

- access control;
- branch protection;
- rulesets;
- secrets;
- environments;
- release permissions;
- issue and discussion settings;
- security settings;
- automation permissions.

Repository administration must follow least privilege.

Administrative access does not automatically grant governance authority.

Departing administrators must have access removed promptly.

Critical access should not depend on a single undocumented account.

---

## 36. Branch Protection

Protected branches should require controls appropriate to their risk.

Controls may include:

- pull-request review;
- required status checks;
- signed commits;
- linear history;
- restricted force pushes;
- restricted deletion;
- release-environment protection;
- code-owner review;
- security review.

Emergency bypasses must be limited, authorized and auditable.

---

## 37. Pull-Request Governance

Pull requests should:

- have a defined purpose;
- remain within declared scope;
- include required tests;
- include documentation changes;
- pass required automation;
- identify breaking changes;
- identify security implications;
- identify migration implications;
- avoid unrelated changes.

Self-approval is not sufficient for significant changes.

Reviewers should evaluate both correctness and alignment with the Constitution.

---

## 38. Architecture Decisions

Material architecture decisions should be recorded.

An architecture decision should identify:

- context;
- decision;
- alternatives;
- consequences;
- affected components;
- compatibility impact;
- security impact;
- operational impact;
- reversal approach.

Architecture records are part of project governance.

Implementation that conflicts with an approved architecture decision requires
review and either:

- implementation correction;
- architecture-record amendment;
- explicit exception.

---

## 39. Product Freeze Governance

A product freeze may be declared during:

- stabilization;
- certification;
- release preparation;
- repository closure;
- security response;
- migration control;
- final audit.

During a product freeze:

- no new product features may be introduced;
- source-code changes require explicit authorization;
- documentation and governance work must remain isolated;
- changed-file scope must be validated;
- HEAD must be verified;
- repository cleanliness must be verified;
- each milestone must end with a controlled commit.

Unexpected repository state is an operational blocker, not permission to change
the roadmap.

The freeze ends only through explicit authorization.

---

## 40. Deprecation Governance

Deprecation decisions must consider:

- user impact;
- migration path;
- replacement capability;
- compatibility period;
- documentation;
- ecosystem dependencies;
- security implications;
- operational cost.

Deprecation does not mean immediate removal.

Removal requires separate authorization unless an urgent security risk makes
continued availability unsafe.

## 45. Code of Conduct Governance

Community conduct is governed by `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`.

Reports should be handled:

- respectfully;
- confidentially where appropriate;
- without retaliation;
- with proportionality;
- with documented authority;
- with conflict-of-interest safeguards.

Serious or repeated violations may result in temporary or permanent restriction

Conduct enforcement must not be used to suppress good-faith technical dissent.

## 46. Appeals

A person materially affected by a governance decision may request review.

An appeal should identify:

- the decision;
- the original authority;
- the reason for appeal;
- relevant evidence;
- requested remedy;
- conflicts of interest.

Appeals should be reviewed by an authority not solely responsible for the
original decision where practical.

An appeal does not automatically suspend an urgent security or safety action.

Final appeal authority rests with the steward unless the Constitution requires
another process.

---

## 47. Dispute Resolution

Technical disputes should be resolved through:

1. direct discussion;
2. evidence and reproducible analysis;
3. module-owner review;
4. lead-maintainer review;
5. TSC review;
6. steward resolution.

Governance disputes should be resolved through:

1. direct discussion;
2. applicable policy interpretation;
3. Governance Council review;
4. steward resolution;
5. constitutional process where required.

Personal attacks and coercion are not acceptable dispute-resolution methods.

---

- review quality;

## 50. Maintainer Removal

A maintainer may be removed or suspended for:

- voluntary resignation;
- prolonged unavailability;
- misuse of authority;
- serious security violations;
- repeated governance violations;
- undisclosed material conflicts;
- code-of-conduct violations;
- unauthorized release activity;
- project abandonment;
- legal necessity.

Except during urgent security or safety incidents, the maintainer should receive:

- notice;
- the relevant concerns;
- an opportunity to respond;
- a documented decision;
- an appeal route.

Repository access may be suspended before final review when necessary to
protect the project.

## 56. Policy Amendments

A governance amendment should include:

- proposed text;
- rationale;
- affected policies;
- compatibility impact;
- authority;
- review period;
- approval record;
- effective date.

Minor amendments may include:

- typographical corrections;
- link corrections;
- formatting;
- clarification without changing authority.

Material amendments include:

- changing voting rights;
- changing steward powers;
- changing release authority;
- changing maintainer appointment rules;
- changing security authority;
- changing appeal rights;
- changing the governance hierarchy.

Material amendments require significant-governance approval.

---

## 57. Constitutional Amendments

A constitutional amendment must not be disguised as an ordinary governance
change.

A constitutional amendment requires:

- explicit identification as constitutional;
- public rationale;
- impact analysis;
- reasonable review period;
- required voting threshold;
- steward approval;
- permanent decision record.

Constitutional amendments should be rare.

---

## 58. Emergency Governance

Emergency governance may be used when normal delay creates material risk.

Emergency authority may address:

- active security exploitation;
- repository compromise;
- credential compromise;
- legal compulsion;
- harmful publication;
- release corruption;
- severe community safety events;
- infrastructure loss.

Emergency actions must be:

- limited in scope;
- time-bound;
- documented;
- reviewed after stabilization;
- reversed when no longer required.

Emergency authority may not permanently amend the Constitution.

---

## 59. No Personal Ownership of Governance Roles

Governance roles belong to the project, not to individuals.

No person may:

- sell a governance role;
- transfer a vote privately;
- inherit project authority automatically;
- use access as evidence of ownership;
- retain credentials after authority ends;
- represent personal decisions as project decisions without authorization.

Contributions remain credited to their authors under applicable licence and
repository records.

## 60. Relationship to Employment

Employment by the steward or another organization does not automatically grant
project authority.

Project authority must be explicitly assigned.

Termination of employment does not automatically determine governance status,
but associated conflicts, access and operational responsibilities must be
reviewed promptly.

Governance decisions should distinguish:

- employment authority;
- corporate authority;
- repository authority;
- project authority.

## 61. Relationship to the Licence

PropertyOS is licensed under the Mozilla Public License 2.0.

Governance controls official project repositories, releases, infrastructure,
identity and community spaces.

The open-source licence continues to govern rights to use, modify and
distribute covered software.

Governance does not remove rights granted under the licence.

Forks may establish their own governance but may not misrepresent themselves as
the official PropertyOS project.
